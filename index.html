01 — About

Generative AI can build a world in minutes. Why not an XR world?

This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners in HCI, eXtended Reality (XR), and AI to explore how generative AI can lower barriers to creating XR experiences. Generative AI can now turn rough ideas into images, videos, and apps within minutes, yet creating XR experiences remains demanding.

XR creations must be grounded in physical space, previewed before they exist, steered through multimodal user inputs, co-created across heterogeneous devices, inspected for AI errors that unfold in interaction, and shared beyond a single headset.

Through keynotes, posters and demos, and hands-on prototyping activities, we will explore these challenges, derive design considerations, and distill a shared research agenda. The outcomes will be published on this website and seed a growing community advancing how generative AI-powered XR can support everyday creativity.

02 — Scope

Six challenges we will work through

Each theme frames open questions that we will refine together and carry into the afternoon prototyping session.

01

In-situ Creation within Physical Space

In-situ XR creation must account for the user's physical surroundings, from coarse properties such as lighting, scale, and object categories down to finer ones such as material and weight.

How can generative models produce content and behaviors that stay physically plausible? What limits how finely creators can author such interactions — sensing, representations, or generative pipelines?
02

Previsualization and Mental Imagery

At the outset, creators picture the result and mentally project it onto their space, yet people differ greatly in how vividly they can do this.

How much should tools previsualize to support imagination without anchoring creators, and in what form? Beyond showing outcomes, how can previews help creators develop their own mental imagery?
03

Expressing and Interpreting Creative Intent

In XR, creators speak with more than words; gesture, gaze, facial expression, and movement all carry meaning.

What creative intent do people convey through these signals, and how should generative AI interpret it? How can creators and AI negotiate ambiguous intent and refine a creation through iteration?
04

Co-Creation across Hardware Heterogeneity

Collaborators rarely hold identical devices; one may wear a headset while another joins from a tablet or phone.

How can generative AI adapt content and interactions across devices to maintain coherent co-creation when input, display, and compute vary widely? What roles suit each device?
05

Evaluating Generated Content

Generative AI inevitably makes mistakes, and in XR such errors are especially hard to inspect and repair — they may surface only through interaction, across viewpoints, and over time.

How can interfaces help creators detect errors and evaluate quality? At which stage of a pipeline should errors be caught, and how can that be made transparent?
06

Cross-Media Creation, Transformation, and Provenance

Existing images, videos, stories, and objects can become interactive XR experiences, while XR creations can be translated into videos or physical artifacts.

What should be preserved, transformed, or newly generated in such translations? As content is remixed, how can provenance, attribution, and ownership remain traceable?
03 — Program

A day built around making things

A full-day, in-person event designed around hands-on interaction and community building.

45 min

Opening and Speed Introductions

Organizers welcome participants and outline the day's goals. Seated in two facing rows, participants introduce themselves one-on-one for two minutes, then rotate to a new partner using a pre-collected introduction slide and ice-breaking prompts.

45 min

Keynote I  Speaker TBA

A 30-minute talk followed by 15 minutes of Q&A. Across the two keynotes we invite speakers representing academia and industry to share their perspectives on XR and generative AI for authoring interactive experiences.

75 min

Poster and Demo Session

Posters and demos selected through the call for participation are set up as stations around the room, where participants try the systems firsthand and talk with their presenters. Each participant carries a short passport to note challenges they encounter and ideas the systems inspire.

—

Lunch break

45 min

Keynote II  Speaker TBA

A second 30-minute talk with 15 minutes of Q&A, establishing shared context for the afternoon's hands-on work.

30 min

Group Discussion

Organizers lay out challenge buckets drawn from the workshop scope, and participants form teams around the topics they find most compelling. Drawing on the keynotes, demos, and their passport notes, each team defines one concrete problem to address.

45 min

Low-Fidelity Prototyping

Each team develops its challenge into a low-fidelity prototype. To enable rapid XR prototyping within limited time, we provide disposable clear face shields as stand-ins for optical see-through displays. Participants draw visual cues directly on the shields and use sketches and sticky notes to represent other modalities such as haptics and audio.

45 min

Closing and Next Steps

Each team gives a five-minute presentation on its outcomes. Organizers summarize the day, outline the post-workshop plan, and invite participants to a persistent community channel.

↳The order above is tentative and exact clock times will be announced closer to the event.

After the workshop

We will consolidate the outcomes into a shared document and publish it here. The document will summarize the challenges explored by each team along with the ideas and scenarios behind the low-fidelity prototypes — a lasting record of the workshop, accessible to the broader community.

04 — Call for Participation

Bring a demo, however early

  • We invite researchers, designers, and practitioners working on XR authoring, generative AI, and creativity support.
  • We equally welcome those applying these technologies in practice — in education, industrial design, and remote collaboration.
  • Interactive demos are especially welcome, whether polished or early-stage, across the full XR spectrum.
  • If you would like to present a demo, please briefly describe your setup and equipment needs so we can coordinate with the venue.
